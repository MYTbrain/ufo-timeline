## Calculation and provenance

`analysis_planetary.js` reuses the existing pinned MIT `analysis_astronomy_engine.js` version 2.1.19. Earth-centered apparent positions use `Ecliptic(GeoVector(body,time,true)).elon`, in the true ecliptic/equinox of date. Aspects are smallest ecliptic-longitude separations, not full 3-D sky separations. A common zodiac-origin shift cancels from aspects. Motion uses an explicitly labelled apparent tropical-longitude rate; near stationary means |rate| ≤ 0.01°/day.

Lahiri uses a mean J2000 reference origin of 23.857092333333334° and the existing MIT engine's precession/nutation transform of the fixed J2000 ecliptic-zero axis. It is labelled “Lahiri convention; Astronomy Engine precession model.” No Swiss Ephemeris library, executable or implementation is bundled. The helper is empirically checked against retained official Swiss calculator outputs across 1582–2100. This is a sampled validation, not a guaranteed global error bound or a byte-identical Swiss implementation.

Public date ordinals explicitly use Unix days. Exact-day records without qualified UTC use noon descriptively and retain the complete possible civil-day interval `[day−14 hours, day+36 hours]`. Interior refinement and small declared guard margins represent boundary/station sensitivity. “Stable/possible” refers to samples and margins; these spans are neither a mathematically certified enclosure nor confidence intervals. Unsupported pre-Gregorian calendars, invalid dates and broad dates remain excluded. Explicit non-UFO context is excluded without assuming a fireball appearance is a known astronomical explanation.

`docs/PLANETARY_METHOD_REVIEW_20261007.md` documents the independent review. `data/analysis_comparisons/planetary_reference_fixtures.json` retains full primary JPL and official sidereal-reference queries/results. Fixtures cover longitude wrapping, tropical and Lahiri sign ingress, Mercury/Venus stations, retrograde and the future Pluto position check. Only small reference data are retained.
