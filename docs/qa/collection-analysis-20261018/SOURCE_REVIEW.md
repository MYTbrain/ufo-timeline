# Collection source review — 18 October 2026

The bounded external pass found one additional public account that postdates the pinned corpus: [NUFORC 198990](https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=198990), a retrospective Cape Town childhood account of suited figures and devices apparently drawing soil upward. It can be retained as an explicitly unmatched web dossier. Its approximate date/time, roughly 38-year reporting delay, obscured faces and absence of independent documentation remain visible. “Direct report” describes the claim, not its verification.

[NUFORC 69295](https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=69295) is another useful review candidate: luminous structures under a triangular object were interpreted as removing matter near Ignacio. There are no described beings and material removal is an analogy. The complementary corpus pass recovered its NUFORC and UPDB representations through exact source IDs. They are two rows of the same account, kept with one source family and an explicit duplication note under ambiguous craft-mediated claims.

Original NUFORC pages also expose two precision mismatches: [177288](https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=177288) and [12256](https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=12256) explicitly mark dates approximate, despite the imported register's `exact_day` value. The supplied register already retains much of their semantic uncertainty. Use source precision when comparing them by date.

The review reaffirmed important distinctions. [Tarlac 50927](https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=50927) describes a craft appendage, with no observed being identity. [St. Florian 19806](https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=19806) describes unspecified figures and apparent cotton removal, with an anonymous source and no further archive investigation. Biological categories must remain unknown where a report does not describe them.

The [official Valensole page](https://geipan.fr/fr/node/46692) still distinguishes early July statements from the August supplement. Its original documents returned HTTP 429 in this pass, so no primary-document access upgrade is claimed. The Pocinhos exact-date join was not resolved: the recovered derivative lead was already in the dossier, and its three-figure version conflicts with the named-witness two-figure summary. Copied retellings add no independent corroboration.

A follow-up to the new water-collection candidates recovered an important negative control: [CUFOS HUMCAT 1950–1951](https://cufos.org/PDFs/HUMCAT/HUMCAT_Index_1950-1951.pdf), PDF page 17, case 50-11, records a Steep Rock Iron Mines representative's 1963 denial of the 1950 story. The cited original letter was not independently recovered, but this archived investigator correspondence reference warrants excluding that account from positive collection comparisons. The separate year-only Atikokan 1960 representation is not automatically merged or classified from geographical similarity.
